Checked and found correct (no findings):
- **Shri Mataji's pronouns** are uppercase throughout (Я/Мені/Мене/Мій/Моєму/Мого/Мною, and «Собою/Своє/Своєму» in the stage directions). The lowercase «я/мені/мене/моїх» occur only in other people's quoted speech: the animals in ¶24, the ego's «Я знаю» and «я» in ¶25, the people's saying in ¶27, the woman's account in ¶45.
- **The interviewer's address to Her** is correct: «Мати, Ваша Сахаджа Йоґа… якщо Ви не вірите» (¶34).
- **Pronouns for Incarnations and God** are correct. Shri Krishna, Christ, Radha, the Father and God take Він/Його/Йому/Вона/Її/Який (¶10–14, 26, 40, 46, 55–56). «Самого Шрі Крішну» (¶55) is capitalized correctly. The plural deities are lowercase mid-sentence («вони пробуджуються… тим доводиться», ¶52). Ordinary people take lowercase: Jung, Ramakrishna, Aurobindo, Muktanand.
- **Glossary terms** match the glossary:
  - Вішуддхі чакра, Кундаліні, Махалакшмі, Радха, Яшода, Бхаґавад Ґіта;

- **Translator's additions in [brackets]** are all minimal and either mirror the EN brackets or bridge spoken gaps without replacing the spoken words: [туди], [Марія], [і], [в концепції Юнга], [ним], [прямий], [частини], [виникає], [знаходиться], [усе це].

### Critic Filter

| Source | # | Verdict | Reason |
|--------|---|---------|--------|
| L | L1 | **Keep** | A subordinate clause that comes before the main clause must be closed by a comma; the dash adds emphasis but does not replace the comma. The transcript itself writes `, –` in the parallel cases ¶16 and ¶31, so ¶27 is an isolated slip. |
| L | L2 | **Keep** | «Якби ви знали…, ви можете мати» is ungrammatical in Ukrainian: «якби» starts an unreal condition and needs «б» in the main clause. AGENTS.md requires the Ukrainian sentence to stay correct Ukrainian even where the spoken English is loose. Changing «можете» → «могли б» is the minimal fix and leaves the content as spoken. |
| L | L3 | **Keep** | A clear punctuation rule (comma between «що» and «якщо» when no «то» follows), applied consistently in earlier reviews and already followed in ¶20 of this text. |
| L | L4 | **Keep** | Feminine «людина» resolved by masculine «він говорив… запитав» in the same sentence is a gender-agreement slip. Earlier reviews (e.g. «така людина… Він знає») set the precedent. «Вона» cannot be used because the EN makes the interviewer male and the paragraph keeps «він» for him later («І він сказав Мені»), so the noun becomes masculine. «один чоловік» (“a certain man”) avoids the «чоловік» = “husband” reading. This is not a content repair: the EN “a person… he” already establishes a man. |
| L | L5 | **Keep** | The comma after «думаючи» shows the translator meant to set the phrase off. A participle phrase after «але» is set off on both sides («але, подумавши, …»). The fix adds the missing opening comma. |
| L | L6 | Remove | False positive. In an etymology passage (¶10–11 explain where the names *Radha* and *Krishna* come from), the name is quoted as a word, and the citation nominative is acceptable. The case forms here are indeclinable names or are already in the instrumental (Христом, ¶13). |
| L | L7 | Remove | Not an error. «бути тим самим, що й…» uses the neuter substantive «те саме» (“the same thing”) as the predicate, which is grammatical for a person too. Varying it with «тією самою» in the next clause is a style choice. |
| L | L8 | Remove | Style preference. Two colons in a sentence are discouraged, not forbidden, and the second colon introduces direct speech, which is fully regular. |
| L | L9 | Remove | Style preference. СУМ-11 gives «вірний» the sense «який відповідає дійсності; правильний», so «вірно» meaning “true” is within the norm. |
| L | L10 | Remove | Not an error. Ukrainian «воно» can serve as the generic “it” (the growing thing), mirroring the EN's equally vague “it”, and the antecedent «коріння» also reads plausibly. Changing it would add the translator's interpretation. |
| L | L11 | Remove | A dash before an adjectival predicate is allowed for logical or intonational emphasis, and here it carries the emphatic “most untraditional”. |
| S | S1 | Remove | Neither name has a glossary entry. «Інтегральна йога» sits inside an editorial gloss and is the established Ukrainian name of Aurobindo's system. The corpus itself is mixed for non-SY yoga names (Хатха Йога ×6 / Хатха Йоґу ×1; Раджа Йоґа ×3 / Раджа Йога ×1). Not an error. |
| S | S2 | Remove | No glossary entry, and the corpus has no fixed convention (the 1977 Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan talk renders “super-conscious” as «надсвідоме»). “super-collective consciousness” and “super-consciousness” are two distinct EN terms, and «над-» is a legitimate rendering of “super-”. Terminology preference, not an error. |
| S | S3 | Remove | False positive. In ¶16, “what we say is the truth” means that the statements about Christ, Krishna and Radha are factually true. It is not the absolute Truth that ¶49 asks us to establish within, which is correctly «Істину». |
| S | S4 | Remove | False positive. The EN has the spoken form “Virat”, and AGENTS.md requires keeping the spoken name. The corpus uses «Вірат» for “Virat” elsewhere too («Вірат – це найви…», «це Вірат»). |
| S | S5 | Remove | False positive. The first «та, що» glosses the syllable *dha* (a generic definition); the second «Та, що» refers to Radha as a Deity, and the capital follows the deity-pronoun rule. |
